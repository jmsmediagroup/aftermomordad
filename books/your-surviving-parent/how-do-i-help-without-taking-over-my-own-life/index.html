Book Six · Your Surviving Parent · Chapter 7

How do I help without taking over my own life?

“Dad calls me four times a day. My brothers live far away. I love him, but I'm drowning. What do other people do?”

After Mom or Dad › The books › Your Surviving Parent › Chapter 7

The short answer

A widowed parent can come to lean on one child for everything. It can be the child who lives nearest, or simply the one who answers the phone.

Help can be shared, and some of it can be paid for. There is job-protected leave for some workers, a caregiver program with respite care, Medicare home health in some cases, and hired help. Your own household and health count too.

This chapter explains how things usually work in the United States. It is not legal, tax or financial advice, and the rules where you live may differ. Before you act on anything here, check it with one of the people named at the end of the chapter.

In the full chapter

The chapter explains what exists and what to ask, then ends with what to do, what can wait so you can set it aside, and who can answer the questions that are yours alone.

The rules, phone numbers and web addresses in this book were checked against the agencies’, courts’ and organizations’ own pages in October 2026. Where a rule differs by state, the book names one state as an example. Check anything you rely on with the office itself.
